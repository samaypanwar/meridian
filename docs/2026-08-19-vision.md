# Meridian — Vision

*Created 2026-08-19. This document states the problem and the thesis. It is the
"why". The spec is the "what"; the architecture is the "how".*

## The problem

I consume information across three modalities — papers, podcasts, books — and
the current process fails in a specific way. There is an unbounded, implicit
queue of open tabs with no traceability to goals, no explicit statement of what
any given source is supposed to yield, and consumption depth chosen by habit
rather than by objective.

The cost shows up twice:

1. **Overload.** The queue lives in working memory, so it generates ambient
   anxiety and arbitrary triage.
2. **Shallow retention.** Sources get read at the wrong abstraction level,
   producing recognition rather than durable understanding.

## The thesis

Strip everything away and the idea is one sentence:

> Consumption is driven by a declared objective per source, and depth and path
> are derived from that objective, not chosen by habit.

Everything else — the queue, the scoring, spaced review — is scaffolding around
that loop. Meridian is a **director**, not a summarizer. Summarization is a
supporting mechanism inside the loop, subordinate to the objective.

## What it is, and what it is not

- It **is** a coach that makes you state why you're reading something, points you
  at what matters given your goals, and makes sure you keep what you learned.
- It **is not** a bookmark pile, a read-it-later app, or a summarizer. Those
  tools store and compress; they do not change how or whether you learn.

## Core design principles

### Objective-first, depth derived
The unit of work is a learning objective per source. Abstraction level and
reading path fall out of the objective; they are never chosen independently.

### The gate is transparency, not friction
Rather than interrogate you at capture time (friction you'll route around), the
agent scores each source against your goals on a small, fixed radar and shows you
the shape. A full radar is on-goal; a thin sliver is curiosity. You decide with
your eyes. Capture stays frictionless; judgment happens when something competes
for one of your bounded active slots.

### Bounded visible queue
The active queue holds ~10 items; the backlog exists but stays out of sight
unless summoned. Unbounded visible queues recreate the open-tabs problem inside
the tool.

### Extractions are irreplaceable; sources are not
Sources are re-fetchable. What you took, why you took it, and what it connects to
are not. The extraction knowledge base is the compounding asset. The target query
it must answer: *"what do I currently believe about X, and on which sources is
that belief based?"*

### Anti-illusion capture
After consuming a source, a forced capture: a few lines of "what did I actually
take from this." Failure to produce it means the level was too shallow; the item
re-queues to be revisited. Re-reading is a placebo; retrieval is the work.

### Interleaving over sequencing
Foundations-before-frontier is a false constraint. A mixed queue at a target
ratio (e.g. 40 foundational / 40 frontier / 20 applied) is the defensible
default, and interleaving aids retention. (Lane-mixing is deferred past the MVP,
but the target ratio lives in the goals doc from day one.)

### Goals are measurable by verb and evidence, not by date
A time horizon is a scheduling axis, not a measurability axis. "Learn RL in six
months" is as unmeasurable as "learn RL". Measurability comes from an observable
action verb plus a success criterion, and from separating proof (lagging Key
Results, human-judged) from input (leading indicators, tool-tracked). Banned
words in objectives: understand, know, learn about, grasp, appreciate. See
`goals.md`.

## The compounding loop

Goals drive scoring. Consumption produces leading indicators — hours by theme,
captures produced, spaced-review pass-rate — which Meridian measures as a
byproduct and reports back against the goals. The goals doc is therefore not a
static file the agent merely reads; it is the thing the whole system reports
progress against. Lagging Key Results stay human-judged; leading indicators are
automatic.

## Why build rather than fork

The read-it-later tools (Karakeep, Wallabag, Linkwarden) are prettier open-tab
queues — capture, tag, search — architected around the exact paradigm this tool
escapes. The second-brain tools (Khoj, SurfSense) sit *after* consumption and
answer "what's in my notes"; they don't shape how you read. Neither does the
director loop, which is the entire point. The commodity plumbing they'd give us
(ingestion, search) is the easy part. So Meridian is built lean, with Obsidian as
the knowledge-base substrate we already own.
